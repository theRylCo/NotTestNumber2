How to Play

Choose a species and guide it through six major threats to biodiversity: habitat destruction, pollution, climate change, overexploitation, invasive species, and disease.

Each level gives you a conservation decision. Correct choices help your species survive. Poor choices reduce population size, habitat quality, genetic diversity, or survival chance.

The goal is not just to avoid extinction. The goal is recovery: a stable, self-sustaining wild population that remains part of its ecological community.

Conservation Lesson

Biodiversity threats rarely act alone. Habitat loss can increase disease spread, fragmentation can block climate-driven range shifts, invasive species can disrupt food webs, and overexploitation can push small populations below recovery.

Real conservation requires monitoring, habitat protection, pollution reduction, sustainable harvest, invasive species prevention, disease management, and long-term recovery planning.

Threat Index

Habitat Destruction: Human alteration of habitat until it is no longer usable by other organisms.

Habitat Degradation: A decrease in habitat quality that lowers individual fitness.

Pollution: Habitat degradation caused by adding harmful substances or conditions.

Climate Change: Long-term shifts in temperature, precipitation, oceans, and seasonal timing.

Overexploitation: Direct human-caused mortality that threatens population viability.

Invasive Species: An established non-native species that causes ecological, economic, or health damage.

Disease: Reduced organism function caused by infectious agents such as bacteria, fungi, viruses, or protists.

Phenology: Timing of seasonal behaviors, such as migration, flowering, hibernation, or breeding.

Bioaccumulation: Toxins build up in the tissues of an organism.

Biomagnification: Toxins become more concentrated higher in the food chain.
